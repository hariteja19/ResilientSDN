## Main findings (honest summary)

* **Simulator validity:** on the 54 points shared with an earlier emulation campaign, Spearman ρ = 0.93
  (time above θ) and 0.86 (loss); same ranking of the three systems in 15 of 18 cells.
* **ResilientSDN vs baselines (seconds a target is above θ, mean over all attack scenarios):** static
  121, ECMP 94, simple reactive 56, k-path 45, **ResilientSDN 35**, TE-LP 24, **v2 15**, TE-Φ 11.
  ResilientSDN makes 2.3 moves per run against 388 for simple reactive; the LPs need fractional splits.
* **The LP baselines beat ResilientSDN** on congestion time (significant in 28–35 of 48 cells, never
  worse). This is reported plainly; the paper frames them as an idealised bound.
* **Internet2 hold reproduced and fixed in simulation:** the sum-of-utilisation drift holds flows when
  only long detours exist (Internet2 S6: 204 s); bottleneck drift (v2) gives 6 s.
* **v2** is significantly better than ResilientSDN in 7 of 48 cells and never worse on congestion time
  or loss; **but its fallback causes secondary congestion** under presaturation (S9: 0.04 → 2.4 links,
  6.7 on Internet2). The ablation shows the bottleneck drift does most of the good and the fallback
  causes most of the harm; severity ordering alone does nothing measurable.
* **Known weaknesses confirmed:** the pulse attack S7 evades the trend detector on every topology
  (ResilientSDN = static); the adaptive attacker S14 keeps a target hot 48–86 s of 120 s.
* **TCP:** victims' mean FCT under overload: static 7.9–17 s, simple reactive 4.5–16 s,
  ResilientSDN/v2 2.0–3.3 s (ideal 2.0 s).
* **Randomised sweep (1,945 instances):** 61 % defendable; defendability falls with α and the number of
  targets (91 % → 23 %), as the cut condition predicts. Median remaining excess (0 = optimal, 1 =
  static): TE-Φ 0.09, **v3 0.40**, k-MP 0.49, v2 0.52, **ResilientSDN 0.70**, simple reactive 1.44.
  v3 beats ResilientSDN on Φ in 1,277 instances, ties 443, loses 225 (Holm p < 1e-100).
  * Pulse attacks with random period/duty: ResilientSDN and v2 median 1.00 (= static), v3 0.27 —
    the pulse loophole of Proposition 3 is general and the duty trigger closes most of it.
  * **New weakness found:** against the routing-aware rolling attacker, ResilientSDN is *worse than
    static routing* in 31 % of rolling instances (TE-Φ 11 %); the attacker follows the rerouted
    victims. Because of this, mean Φ over the sweep is higher for ResilientSDN than static (134.5 vs
    119.8); without rolling instances 40.3 vs 60.0. Reported in the paper and limitations.
  * **Ranking differs from the hand-made scenarios in one place:** k-path multipath is ahead of
    ResilientSDN at the median (0.49 vs 0.70) but worse than static routing in 32 % of instances.

## Incidents found and fixed during the day

1. **CPU contention corrupted emulation.** The first WAN-extension attempt ran beside the simulator;
   loss rose to 5–10 % and stats polling was starved. Those 30 runs are in
   `results/wan_ext_CONTENDED_do_not_use/` (with a README) and were re-run on an idle host.
2. **TE-LP non-determinism** (LP variable order from a Python `set`; depends on `PYTHONHASHSEED`).
   Fixed by sorting; all TE-LP runs recomputed; regression test added.
3. **Min-max TE degeneracy on GÉANT** (one 1.55 Mbps link pins U* = 3.23, so the LP never relieves the
   target in 12/30 runs). Diagnosed, documented, and TE-Φ added.
4. **Laptop sleep stopped the emulation** for several hours (the WSL VM restarts on resume). The
   runner is resumable, so no run was lost or duplicated; `docker/keep_awake.ps1` now holds a wake lock
   (no power settings changed) while a campaign runs. Keep the lid open and the charger connected.
